Working below the spillway

Tehri HPP (1,000 MW) and Tehri PSP only stop for short, scheduled shutdowns. Each CPS job had to fit inside one.

Below the dam the Bhagirathi rose and fell 8–10 m twice a day, leaving 3–4 hours of working time per shutdown. Inside the tunnels the only access was a crane down a 100 m surge shaft.

River dredging, phase 1

Feb – Jul 2023Zero schedule of Tehri HPP

Dredging across 640 Rm of the Bhagirathi downstream of the spillway, done while the river level swung 8–10 m twice a day.

  • Dredging to THDCIL’s daily zero schedule
  • ~1 lakh cum dredged and hauled 14–15 km
  • 12 excavators (210) and 2 long-boom excavators (300)
  • 50–60 dumpers for muck transport
  • 12 dewatering pumps, 50 HP each, 7,000–8,000 LPM
  • Dedicated 30 cum batching plant and 6 transit mixers
  • All resources deployed 24 hours in two shifts

Phase 1 dredging, photographed

Baffle wall and micro piling

Feb – Jul 202347 m across the Bhagirathi

A baffle wall about 47 m long across the river between the spillway and the TRT of Tehri HPP, poured under water onto a micro-piled, saturated riverbed.

  • ~2,000 cum RCC in 4 months
  • 1,000 mm dia Hume pipe through the wall
  • 700 m of micro piling in saturated foundation, ~400 Rm in one month
  • Built in 5–10 m segments
  • Underwater concreting by mobile boom placer
  • Drilling and grouting of anchors on both banks

Building the wall

River dredging, phase 2

Jun – Jul 2024A 35-day shutdown

The second phase dredged 1,50,000 cum and built 70 m ramps either side of the baffle wall, all inside a 35-day shutdown and through monsoon rain.

  • 1,50,000 cum dredged from chainage 260 m to 660 m
  • ILO discharge never stopped; water never below EL 601.5 m
  • A 12-day approach road built through the river
  • A 12 m deep area found across the full river width
  • 20 more dumpers brought in mid-job
  • Emergency wall for an exposed THDCIL building foundation

Phase 2 on the riverbed

Ramps and micro piling, phase 2

Jun – Jul 202414,400 cum concreted

Ramps 70 m long and 5 m wide along the Bhagirathi, piled and poured under water in the same shutdown.

  • 70 m ramps, 5 m wide, upstream and downstream
  • ~850 m of micro piling in the ramp foundation
  • Underwater concrete by boom placer
  • Built in 5–10 m segments because of seepage
  • Foundation held at EL 601 m against a target of EL 598–600 m
  • Shuttering fixed in standing water; buried formwork dug out

Tehri PSPSeepage control

Morning glory shaft epoxy treatment

Shafts T1 to T4Four months

Repair of the swirling-device bottom in all four surge shafts, with water falling continuously from 150–160 m above. A 25 m scaffold went up inside each 16 m shaft and the water was funnelled away from the work.

  • 25 m scaffold built inside a 16 m shaft
  • Water falling 150–160 m turned aside with custom GI sheets
  • Epoxy treatment, mortar seals and fibreglass wrap

Epoxy treatment, intakes 1 and 2

31 May – 14 Jun 202516 days against 30 planned

Epoxy treatment at Intake Structures 1 and 2, EL 745 m, Tehri HPP. Planned for 30 days, handed over in 16.

  • Epoxy mortar at construction joints
  • Old cement concrete wearing coat removed
  • Epoxy paint on steel; steel work in the gates
  • Bitumen felt removed over joints
  • Four-course waterproofing with bitumen felt
  • Stainless steel plates fixed over the felt
